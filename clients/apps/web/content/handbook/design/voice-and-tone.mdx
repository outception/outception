---
title: 'Voice & Tone'
description: 'How Outception writes'
---

Outception sounds like a well-run newsroom front page: direct, current, no
editorialising about itself.

## Rules

**Lead with the thing.** The first line of a headline, a setting, an error or
an email carries the point. Everything after it supports it.

**Short sentences.** More than two clauses means split it.

**Active voice.** "Outception shows the news", not "the news is shown".

**Specific over general.** "Headlines from 2,500+ sources" beats "comprehensive
coverage".

**Cut needless words.** If the sentence works without a word, remove it.

**Don't explain the obvious.** Readers know what a headline is. Labels are
nouns: "Sources", "Deck", "Edition".

**No performed enthusiasm.** No exclamation marks and no greetings in UI copy.

**One term per concept.** It is a "deck" of "sources" grouped by "topic",
everywhere, in every locale.

## The product never speaks for the news

Outception presents headlines from their sources and links to them. UI copy
does not summarise, rate or comment on stories. The source's name is always
visible next to its headline.
